## Design Context

### Users

This is a trilingual learning map for people who want to understand or build AI agents. The primary reader may be starting from zero and needs to know what to learn first, what one small action to try, and where to find trustworthy official or high-quality material. Researchers, developers, teachers, knowledge workers, and everyday users later branch into role-specific paths without losing the shared route.

### Brand Personality

Calm, direct, and encouraging. Explanations should feel as clear as teaching a five-year-old without sounding childish: use plain words first, then preserve and define the exact technical term. The project should help readers feel oriented and capable, never impressed by density for its own sake.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a light, bright, editorial learning-map style aligned with the README: cream or softly tinted surfaces, dark navy text, and a small set of clear blue, orange, green, and purple accents. Diagrams should be simple, spacious, aligned, and immediately readable. Avoid dark neon AI styling, decorative gradients, overlapping arrows or icons, repeated generic card grids, and visual metrics that become stale.

### Design Principles

1. Show the learning spine first: purpose, core terms, one next action, required reading, curated projects, and completion check stay visible.
2. Collapse only secondary setup, time, cost, alternatives, deep implementation detail, and troubleshooting; never hide required reading or full rated learning resources.
3. Define every important term in plain language, bold it at first visible use, and keep the correct English term so readers can find official documentation.
4. Preserve progressive depth instead of deleting concepts: move advanced material behind a clear disclosure and leave its title, outcome, or route visible when readers need the landmark.
5. Keep tables quiet and scannable: group like items, merge repeated category cells with real row groups, preserve five-star editorial ratings, and state status or limits without unstable popularity metrics.
6. Treat text as the canonical navigation. A diagram may reinforce the route but may not be the only way to understand or follow it.
7. Maintain semantic headings, meaningful alt text, keyboard-safe native disclosures, readable line lengths, responsive tables, and equivalent meaning across zh-TW, English, and zh-Hans.
